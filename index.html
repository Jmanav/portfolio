SkinSure

A clinical decision-support prototype for skin-disease screening — ensemble classification, explainability, federated learning, and an agentic LLM layer, documented end to end with measured results and open gaps.

Overview & Provenance

SkinSure is a clinical decision-support prototype for skin-disease screening, built specifically for Fitzpatrick IV–VI skin tones (the project’s stated target, underrepresented in most public dermatology datasets). It is explicitly not a diagnostic device — the product framing throughout the documentation is “a screening aid that says ‘see a doctor’ readily.”

The system is a six-stage pipeline: image in → preprocessing → a 3-model deep learning ensemble → Grad-CAM explainability → an LLM-based clinical summarizer → deterministic severity triage → structured output out. A separate, parked track adds federated learning as an alternative training regime. A further, largely-planned track (“A0–A9”, the Production Agent Engineering plan) covers observability, evals, guardrails and LLMOps for the agentic layer.

Provenance note for this write-up. Every fact below is sourced from the repo’s own markdown documentation (CLAUDE.md, docs/ROADMAP.md, docs/architecture.md, fedler.md, paper/PUBLISHABLE_FINDINGS.md, src/models/CLAUDE.md, docs/agentops/adr/*, docs/agentops/A0_baseline.md, the Production Agent Engineering Track plan, and paper/new_results.md), which the project instructions mandate as the primary source of truth. Numbers are quoted exactly as stated in those files — not rounded, softened, or reconciled across files where the files themselves flag unresolved discrepancies (these are called out explicitly below). No source code was read to produce this document; every section is markdown-sourced, and that is noted again inline wherever a figure is unusually load-bearing.

Stage 1 — Data ingestion & preprocessing Partial

Quality gate + 3 pixel-changing steps built and measured; all 3 ship OFF.

Goal

Clean and gate incoming images before they reach the model, and enforce the Fitzpatrick IV–VI population target at the metadata boundary.

What was built

  • src/preprocessing/: quality_check.py (reject <200px short side or near-featureless; warn on borderline blur), hair_removal.py (DullRazor-style black-hat + inpainting), artifact_removal.py (ruler/ink/bubble inpainting), color_normalization.py (Shades-of-Gray), pipeline.py (preprocess() orchestrator), inference_transform.py (224px resize + ImageNet normalisation, used by every inference path).
  • preprocess(image) orients via EXIF, runs the quality gate, then runs enabled pixel-changing steps in order hair → artifacts → colour, returning a PreprocessResult (image, quality, steps_applied, notes). A rejected image returns quality.ok == False with patient-readable quality.reasons.
  • Thresholds were derived from a 1,500-image sample of the raw dataset: short side ≥ 200px; blur (Laplacian variance) rejects below 5, warns below 15. A higher blur cutoff was explicitly rejected because it would reject BCC and Melanoma images far more often than Eczema/Tinea (~18%/9% vs ~1%/0.3% at 15) — a fairness-by-class tradeoff documented directly in CLAUDE.md.
  • The Fitzpatrick IV–VI gate is enforced in api/schemas/patient.py (PatientMetadata), not in the image pipeline: a validator rejects Fitzpatrick I–III before anything reaches the model. An image-based skin_tone_classifier.py is explicitly future scope, not built.
  • tests/unit/ covers preprocessing on synthetic images only (gate boundaries, guards, step order, defaults-off). No test validates tuning on real images.

Tech stack

OpenCV (cv2) for blur/morphology/inpainting, Pillow/NumPy for image I/O, Pydantic v2 for the metadata schema and gate, PyYAML config (configs/preprocessing.yaml) for step switches and thresholds.

Key design decisions

  • All three pixel-changing steps ship OFF by default, because the shipped v2 ensemble was trained on resize + ImageNet-normalisation only — any pixel-mutating preprocessing is a train/inference mismatch unless separately justified by measurement.
  • Hair removal (ADR-F5): measured harmful, stays off. Full paired A/B over the clean 565-image test set (scripts/hair_ab_experiment.py / _analyze.py, raw rows in .nb1out/hair_ab_raw.csv). It corrected zero images and broke two — both true Basal Cell Carcinoma: ISIC_0054063 BCC→0.9314 → Keratosis→0.3366 (a confident malignant call turned benign), ISIC_0073116 BCC→0.6303 → Melanoma→0.3881. Whole-set accuracy −0.35pp, macro AUC −0.0011, BCC sensitivity 100%→98.11%; melanoma sensitivity exactly unchanged (121/124 both arms). McNemar b=2, c=0, exact p=0.50 (not significant at n=2, but the sign is unanimous). Root cause (not just an effect): at threshold=10 the black-hat filter is non-selective on this dataset — of 524 images the MAX_MASK_FRACTION guard blocked, the mask covered a median 58.9% of the frame (≥75% on 185 of them); the 41 images (7.26%) that slipped under the 10% guard inpainted a median 6.9% of pixels, sitting just under the cutoff rather than being precise hair masks. Firing concentrated on BCC (24.5% firing rate) because BCC lesions are dark/textured and respond to black-hat like hair does — the step preferentially damages the class most dangerous to damage. Determinism control: 524 skipped images showed max probability shift of 0.00000, confirming both flips are genuine inpainting effects, not noise.
  • Artifact removal (ADR-F6): measured inert, stays off. Same harness/statistics, same 565 images. Fired on 335/565 (59.3%) — eight times more often than hair removal — yet changed exactly one label, a fix: v-tinea-face-4, Keratosis(0.4655)→Tinea(0.4882), a coin-flip margin. Accuracy +0.18pp (87.61%→87.79%), macro AUC −0.0001. Both safety-critical sensitivities bit-identical: melanoma 121/124 and BCC 106/106 in both arms, McNemar b=0,c=0 on each. McNemar overall b=0, c=1, p=1.0000. Masks were precise where hair removal’s were not: median 0.31% of frame on fired images (vs hair’s 6.9%), and the MAX_MASK_FRACTION guard never tripped on BCC (0/106). Conclusion (ADR-F6): “measured, harmless, and pointless” — a single discordant pair cannot justify adding a pixel-mutating step the models were never trained with. The contrast between F5 and F6 is itself a documented finding (PUBLISHABLE_FINDINGS F6): the discriminating variable between a dangerous and a harmless preprocessing step is mask precision, not firing rate or the mere presence of preprocessing.
  • Colour normalisation (Shades-of-Gray): off by default; noted as a risk because it can flatten genuine skin-tone colour, which matters directly for a Fitzpatrick IV–VI target population.

Gaps/limitations

  • Preprocessing tuning has never been validated against real images — only synthetic images (gate boundaries) and the two A/B experiments above (which test whether to enable a step, not quality-check threshold tuning on real photos).
  • No annotated hair masks exist to re-tune the hair-removal kernel; a fix would need a thinner, elongation-aware kernel validated against hair-annotated images, which does not exist in this project and is not planned.
  • augmentation.py (training-time, tone-aware augmentation) is listed in the target architecture but not created.
  • skin_tone_classifier.py (automatic Fitzpatrick detection from the image) is explicit future scope, not planned for the core product — the IV–VI gate today relies solely on patient-reported metadata.

Stage 2 — Ensemble model architecture & training Completed

3 backbones trained, retrained, verified; ensemble weighting analysed and resolved as a null result.

Goal

Classify a skin lesion image into 5 clinically relevant classes (Basal_Cell_Carcinoma, Eczema, Keratosis, Melanoma, Tinea) with sensitivity weighted toward malignancies, using an ensemble of complementary architectures.

What was built

Three independently trained backbones, combined by weighted softmax averaging:

Backbonetimm namefeat_dimRole (per CLAUDE.md)
EfficientNet-B1efficientnet_b11280texture
ConvNeXt-Tinyconvnext_tiny768varied lighting
Swin-Tinyswin_tiny_patch4_window7_224768global lesion context

All three use an identical head: LayerNorm → Dropout → Linear(feat,256) → GELU → Dropout/2 → Linear(256,5). LayerNorm (not BatchNorm) was deliberately chosen for the head because EfficientNet’s original BatchNorm1d head required batch_size > 1 and failed silently without model.eval().

src/models/ensemble.py (SkinEnsemble, CheckpointPaths) loads the three backbones and averages their softmax outputs with weights from configs/models.yaml (shipped: equal 1/1/1). Predictions.agreement_score = fraction of the three models whose top-1 matches the ensemble top-1 (0, 0.33, 0.67, 1.0); confidence_tier() maps this to high/moderate/low. The ensemble refuses to load checkpoints whose stored classes are not exactly the 5-class set, and there is no fallback — get_checkpoint_paths() returns exactly the three configured v2 paths; a missing checkpoint raises rather than silently degrading.

Tech stack

PyTorch, timm (pretrained backbones), Pydantic v2 for the Predictions/XAISummary/ClinicalSummary schema contracts, YAML config for checkpoint paths and ensemble weights.

Training recipe (per src/models/CLAUDE.md)

  • Checkpoint selection criterion, all three backbones: composite = 0.4×melanoma_sensitivity + 0.3×BCC_sensitivity + 0.3×val_acc — saves the model that maximises malignant recall, not raw accuracy.
  • Dual class-imbalance handling: WeightedRandomSampler at the batch level, plus CrossEntropyLoss weighting with clinical boosts (Melanoma ×2, BCC ×2, Tinea ×3, Eczema ×1.5) on top of 1/√count base weights.
  • Freeze-then-finetune schedule: head-only freeze phase, then full-model finetuning at backbone LR = head LR × 0.1. Swin uses a longer freeze (originally 8 epochs) and lower base LR (1e-4) for stability.
  • Actual 5-class corpus: 6,655 images total (train 5,180 / val 748 / test 727, ≈78/11/11), near-balanced (max/min ratio 1.24:1) — explicitly not the older, now-superseded 14.5k-image / 5:1-imbalance figures still referenced in some older files.

The v1 → v2 retrain — why it happened and what changed

The three original (“v1”) checkpoints (*_5cls_skin.pth) did not reproduce their own stored metrics when reloaded and re-scored (the save-then-verify gate failed). All three were retrained (“v2”) with a verified save-then-verify gate (torch.equal on every tensor; re-score within < 1e-9 of the stored test_acc). The app loads only the v2 checkpoints; v1 and an older bare-state-dict set (~479MB total) were deleted 2026-11-02, were gitignored (not recoverable from git), and are documented as requiring a retrain if ever needed.

Measured on the 727-image contaminated paper test set, the retrain shifted backbone rankings substantially:

Backbonev1 accuracyv2 accuracyΔ
EfficientNet-B185.11%84.73%−0.38pp
Swin-Tiny85.39%85.97%+0.58pp
ConvNeXt-Tiny71.35%89.13%+17.78pp

ConvNeXt’s v1 weakness was a training-schedule artifact, not an architectural one: it had received roughly one epoch of effective fine-tuning (freeze_epochs 8, patience 5, num_epochs 30 stopped it early). With freeze_epochs 3, patience 10, num_epochs 40, the same architecture becomes the strongest single backbone, beating Swin by 3.17pp. This reversal invalidates the original paper’s ensemble-complementarity argument (“ConvNeXt is the weak link the ensemble compensates for”) — paper/new_results.md explicitly withdraws that argument.

On the de-duplicated, primary-reporting 565-image clean test set: EfficientNet 84.25%, Swin 85.66%, ConvNeXt 88.67%, and the shipped equal-weight 3-model ensemble (“ECS”) scores 87.79% accuracy, macro-AUC 0.9813.

Three test sets exist and must never be compared with each other (explicit invariant in both CLAUDE.md and PUBLISHABLE_FINDINGS.md): the 997-image FL split (fedler.md), the 727-image contaminated paper test set, and the 565-image de-duplicated clean set (727 minus 162 near-duplicates found by pHash, 22.3% — the primary reporting basis for paper claims).

Key design decision: ensemble weighting (ADR-F1) — keep equal weights

A grid search over 1,033 weight vectors on the clean 565-set found an EfficientNet+Swin (“ES”, dropping ConvNeXt) configuration reaching 99.19% melanoma sensitivity vs the shipped ECS’s 97.58% — a 1.6pp gap on the single most safety-critical metric. This was tested for significance and did not survive: only 2 discordant images, McNemar exact p=0.50, Wilson 95% CIs overlap substantially (ECS [93.13,99.17]% vs ES [95.57,99.86]%). Pooling test+validation to 326 melanoma images gives 5 unanimously ES-favouring discordances — perfectly one-sided but the best achievable p is 0.0625 (need ≥6 for α=0.05); reaching 80% power at this effect size needs ~1,500–2,000 melanoma images against 124 (or 326 pooled) available. Meanwhile the better-powered statistics favour ECS: macro AUC 0.9813 vs 0.9772, and switching to ES costs a significant ~2pp accuracy (test p=0.043, validation p=0.020) and regresses BCC sensitivity −3.36pp on validation. Decision: keep equal weights — “paying a measured cost for an unmeasured benefit is the wrong trade.” This is published as a null result with a power analysis, framed as a corrective to ablation tables that rank configurations on fractions of a percent without testing whether the ranking is statistically distinguishable.

Key design decision: ranked-melanoma escalation (ADR-F4) — built, shipped OFF

While investigating F1, it was discovered that all three melanomas the ECS ensemble misses share one shape: melanoma ranks high but is not the argmax (p(melanoma) = 0.343, 0.369, 0.064). The existing severity-triage safety rule gates on primary_label (the argmax), so no threshold adjustment of that rule can ever catch these cases — a structural blind spot invisible to melanoma-sensitivity metrics computed on argmax predictions. A new rule (“F4”) was built that reads the full class_probabilities distribution and escalates when p(melanoma) ≥ threshold regardless of rank. Threshold 0.31 was selected on the 748-image validation split (zero-cost plateau 0.31–0.35, recovering all 3 non-argmax validation melanomas at zero false escalations); applied to test it recovers 2 of 3 known misses for 1 false escalation (0.23% of non-melanoma). Decision: ship the code, leave the behaviour OFF — 3 validation and 3 test melanomas cannot establish this is a net safety gain at this sample size, even though the rule only ever escalates or blocks, never downgrades.

Measured clinical sensitivity targets (src/models/CLAUDE.md targets: Melanoma ≥95%, BCC ≥90%, Tinea/Eczema/Keratosis ≥80%)

EfficientNet-B1 v2 retrain, n=727: BCC 99.17% ✓, Melanoma 98.40% ✓, Eczema 82.76% ✓, Tinea 81.74% ✓, Keratosis 61.39% ✗ (−18.6pp). NPV Melanoma 0.9944, BCC 0.9983 (both ≥0.95 ✓).

Gaps/limitations

  • The Keratosis regression is confirmed across all three independently retrained architectures (EfficientNet 61.39%, Swin 68.35%, ConvNeXt 74.68% — all below the ≥80% target). It is attributed to the composite checkpoint criterion itself: Keratosis enters only through the diluted 0.3 val_acc term and carries the lowest loss weight of the five classes (0.1079, no clinical boost) — i.e. this is the intended effect of optimising for malignant recall, not a bug, and it is the same class that regressed hardest under federated learning (70.10%→56.70%).
  • Model layer: losses.py and metrics.py are listed in the target architecture but not confirmed present in the current implementation state; training exists primarily as Colab notebooks plus src/training/train_utils.py (extracted for federated learning, see Stage 4).
  • Ensemble-level accuracy/macro-AUC/MCC/ablation numbers from the paper’s own evaluation notebook (NB1) were, as of the source documents, either pending or superseded by the clean-565 re-scoring; paper/new_results.md flags that nothing about the ensemble itself (as opposed to single backbones) was independently re-verified from raw predictions at the time of writing for ConvNeXt specifically (its results archive was not received; its numbers are verified-arithmetic, not verified-artefact).

Stage 3 — Explainable AI (xAI) layer Partial

Grad-CAM implemented, run on real photos, and faithfulness-tested; the heatmap→summary bridge is unvalidated; SHAP/LIME not built.

Goal

Show which part of an image drove the prediction, and convert that into a structured, patient-facing explanation (XAISummary) consumed by the clinical summarizer.

What was built

  • src/explainability/gradcam.py: grad_cam, ensemble_heatmap, with per-backbone target layers (bn2 for EfficientNet, stages[-1] for ConvNeXt, layers[-1] for Swin — Swin requires an NHWC transpose).
  • xai_summary.py: summarize_heatmap() converts a heatmap → XAISummary (focus_description: lesion_centre/lesion_border/diffuse/background_skin/artifact/unknown; boundary_alignment: good/partial/poor/unknown; artifact_flag) via estimate_lesion_mask(), a brightness segmentation, not a trained segmenter.
  • explainer.py: GradCamExplainer, the default explainer create_app() wires in when a real ensemble is used and none is injected. With no explainer available, /predict returns 503 rather than inventing explainability data.
  • evaluation.py: deletion-curve / AOPC faithfulness scorer, usable against Grad-CAM, any external heatmap (e.g. SHAP .npy), or a random baseline under one identical procedure (scripts/evaluate_xai.py).

Tech stack

PyTorch forward/backward hooks (no third-party Grad-CAM library — custom per-backbone hooks), OpenCV/NumPy for heatmap post-processing, Pydantic for the XAISummary schema (with a validator requiring artifact_flag=True whenever focus is artifact).

Key design decision: Grad-CAM over SHAP on /predict — latency and output-shape, not faithfulness

The documentation is explicit that this was never a faithfulness claim until measured (see below). The stated reasons for choosing Grad-CAM on the live path:

  • Latency: Grad-CAM is one forward + one backward pass per backbone (~2.6–6.3s mean, up to 15.7s measured on 15 real photos on the dev CPU — an earlier, often-quoted 2.3s figure came from a single synthetic image and was wrong). SHAP’s patch/kernel methods need hundreds–thousands of forward passes, i.e. minutes on CPU, and /predict is synchronous.
  • Output shape: Grad-CAM yields a dense 7×7 spatial map natively; SHAP yields per-patch attributions needing their own conversion to a spatial map.
  • Already solved for all three backbones, including Swin’s NHWC transpose.

Faithfulness result (ADR-F7 / PUBLISHABLE_FINDINGS F7) — measured, not assumed

On a Kaggle T4, AOPC (Area Over the Perturbation Curve — progressively delete the top-k% most-attributed pixels, measure the drop in p(predicted class)) was run on 150 images from the clean 565-set, 30 per class, all five classes:

MethodMean AOPC95% CI
Grad-CAM0.3099[0.2831, 0.3376]
random7x7 (structure-matched baseline)0.1834[0.1678, 0.1994]
randompixel (sanity-check baseline)0.4887[0.4576, 0.5196]

Grad-CAM − random7x7 = +0.1266 [+0.1059, +0.1478], Wilcoxon p<0.0001, 127/150 wins (84.7%), Cohen’s dz=0.967. Significant in all five per-class rows (p≤0.0012), and it holds on images the ensemble got wrong (+0.1479, p=0.0006) as well as right — faithfulness is a property of the explanation, not of correctness.

The methodological trap found in the same study: randompixel (i.i.d. pixel-level noise) posts the highest AOPC of all three arms despite zero localisation content, because scattering deletions everywhere shreds texture and pushes the image off-distribution — it measures model fragility to corruption, not explanation quality. Only the structure-matched random7x7 baseline is a valid faithfulness comparison. This is documented explicitly as a trap in the metric itself: “an AOPC comparison against an unmatched random baseline can report the opposite of the truth.”

A bug found and fixed in the same analysis: the script initially used the raw (1,1,1) ensemble weights from config rather than the normalised (1/3,1/3,1/3) SkinEnsemble actually uses, making every probability 3× too large. Verified harmless — AOPC is a linear functional of the deletion curve, so win-count (127/150) and dz (0.967) are bit-identical before and after; only the magnitudes were wrong.

What this does and does not settle: it shows Grad-CAM beats chance on these models (the maps are not decorative), which reframes the unvalidated XAISummary bridge as a thresholding problem, not a “model ignores the lesion” problem. It says nothing about SHAP — that comparison is explicitly deferred by decision (2026-11-03), not pending or blocked: SHAP is not installed, /explain (where it would serve) is not built, and nothing in the product currently waits on the comparison. A teammate’s SHAP prototype exists on branch xai-shap (two Colab notebooks, not merged, not importable) with AOPC figures that used a different blanking baseline in each notebook — not comparable until re-scored with evaluate_xai.py.

Real-photo validation (CLAUDE.md gap 6)

Run on 15 real test photos (3 per class). Pipeline worked end-to-end with no crashes; artifact_flag fired on 0/15 (no spurious retakes). v2 checkpoints: 11/15 correct vs 12/15 on v1 (noise at this n), but confidence rose markedly (BCC 0.72→0.958). Latency was 2.6–6.3s mean, up to 15.7s — the figure that invalidated the earlier 2.3s synthetic-image estimate used elsewhere in planning documents.

Gaps/limitations

  • The heatmap→XAISummary bridge is heuristic and unvalidated. Over the 15 real photos, boundary_alignment was poor 9 times, partial 4, unknown 2, and never good — “a field that never reaches its best value across a whole class spread is more likely mis-thresholded than right.” focus_description was background_skin on 4/15, including 2 correct high-confidence Melanoma calls (0.957, 0.853).
  • Annotated lesion masks are explicitly out of scope (decided 2026-11-03) — the project does not have them and will not acquire them, so boundary_alignment/focus_description can never be validated as correct about the lesion location, only as advisory signals. AOPC answers “did the map find pixels the model used,” not “are those pixels the lesion” — a different question this project cannot answer.
  • artifact_flag’s real-world trigger rate is unmeasured beyond n=15 (0/15 fired). The documented concern: if the flag is itself mis-tuned, a flagged patient’s retake could be flagged again, looping to no answer — clinically close to a missed melanoma. The recommended mitigation (watch the recapture-loop rate in production) depends on the observability work in Stage 7, which is largely unimplemented.
  • SHAP (shap_explainer.py) and LIME (lime_explainer.py) are not implemented — listed in the target architecture as planned extensions (ROADMAP Phase 11), deliberately deferred.

Stage 4 — Federated learning (parked) Partial, PARKED 2026-11-02

Phases 0–2 of 5 complete; differential privacy and multi-backbone FL not started; a data-integrity bug found before any re-run.

Goal

Train the ensemble across simulated hospital clients without any raw patient image leaving its origin institution, as a response to India’s DPDP Act and basic patient-privacy requirements — motivated as a B2B product track (hospitals as FL clients) distinct from the B2C inference path.

What was built (Phases 0–2 of the fedler.md plan)

  • src/training/train_utils.py: reusable training loop (train_one_epoch, evaluate, create_model, build_loss_weights, build_optimizer, build_scheduler, transforms, dataloaders) extracted from the Colab notebooks, supporting all three backbones.
  • src/federated/client.py: SkinSureClient, a Flower NumPyClient subclass representing one hospital, plus a ClientApp.
  • src/federated/server.py: weighted_average metric aggregation plus a configurable FedAvg strategy wrapped in a ServerApp.
  • scripts/run_fl_simulation.py: standalone simulation via fl.simulation.start_simulation() with FedAvgWithCheckpoint (captures aggregated parameters for post-training evaluation).
  • scripts/make_federated_shards.py: generates both IID and non-IID 3-hospital shards (Hospital A: Melanoma/BCC-heavy urban center; Hospital B: Tinea/Eczema-heavy tropical clinic; Hospital C: mixed).
  • scripts/train_baseline.py: centralized baseline trainer with early stopping and composite-score tracking.
  • Canonical split manifests under data/splits/.

Tech stack

Flower (flwr) for the FL framework (ClientApp/ServerApp/NumPyClient, new-API flwr run pattern, not the legacy start_server/start_client), PyTorch for the underlying models, run as a single-machine simulation — no real multi-process networking (Phase 3) and no real hospital partnerships exist.

Key design decisions

  • Hospital-level clients, not per-patient. A SkinSure patient contributes only 5–10 images — treating each patient as an FL client would spend a full communication round on a training signal too small to matter. Hospitals/clinics as clients is the canonical FL use case and the privacy story (data genuinely cannot leave the institution) is real under DPDP.
  • EfficientNet-B1 only for the first pass (Phase 0–2); ConvNeXt and Swin are deliberately deferred to Phase 4 so FL mechanics are debugged on one backbone first.
  • Independent FL per backbone (Phase 4 design, not yet executed): each backbone is a standalone classifier with no shared parameters, so each would federate as 3 separate FL jobs; ensemble weights remain an inference-time concern outside FL aggregation.
  • Differential privacy deliberately deferred to Phase 5: DP noise would obscure whether a bug is “my FL code” or “DP epsilon too aggressive,” so a noise-free FedAvg baseline had to exist first.

Measured results — centralized baseline vs. federated (EfficientNet-B1, FedAvg, 10 rounds, non-IID, 3 hospitals), both on the held-out global test set

MetricCentralizedFederatedGap
Test accuracy83.15%79.94%−3.21pp
Melanoma sensitivity98.32%97.06%−1.26pp

Per-class: the accuracy loss concentrates almost entirely in Keratosis (70.10%→56.70%, −13.4pp); the safety-critical classes hold — Melanoma sensitivity stays within 1.3pp of centralized at 99.60% specificity. Tinea actually improved under FL (80.56%→83.33%, +2.77pp), consistent with the non-IID design concentrating Tinea training signal at Hospital B.

Gaps/limitations — both found during audit, before any further training

  • Integer-buffer averaging bug (affects EfficientNet, not Swin/ConvNeXt). SkinSureClient.get_parameters() ships all state_dict values including EfficientNet’s 69 int64 num_batches_tracked buffers; FedAvg averages them as floats and set_parameters() truncates them back to int64. Documented as not having corrupted the Phase 2 results (the stats that affect inference — running_mean/running_var — are floats and average correctly), but it is wrong and must be fixed before Phase 3/4 since ConvNeXt and Swin have zero non-float buffers and all three backbones must run on identical, correct plumbing for a fair comparison.
  • Split-manifest mismatch, found 2026-11-03. The documented corpus is 997-image test / 5,658-image trainable pool, but the manifest CSVs on disk hold 904 test / 5,131 pool (total 6,035, not 6,655) — 620 images across exactly three classes (Eczema 189, Keratosis 151, Melanoma 280) appear in no manifest at all. Root cause verified directly: scripts/make_splits.py’s n_test = max(1, int(len(items) * 0.15)) reproduces both the 904 figure (applied to the manifest’s own per-class totals) and the 997 figure (applied to the true disk totals) exactly — i.e. the manifests were almost certainly built against an earlier, smaller snapshot of the dataset and never regenerated after three classes were topped up. Consequence: every FL and baseline metric in this section was almost certainly measured on the 904-image set, not 997, and should not be cited as the 997-image figure until the splits are regenerated and reconfirmed. This is recorded as a fix to make before any Phase 3/4 re-run, not a defect in the numbers reported above as measurements of something real.
  • Federated learning is parked entirely as of 2026-11-02, pending re-run on a different dataset than the one the numbers above were measured on — explicitly separated from ongoing product work. Every FL number in this section (and in fedler.md) is to be treated as pending re-measurement.
  • Not built at all: Phase 3 (real multi-process/networked FL, vs. today’s single-machine simulation), Phase 4 (ConvNeXt/Swin federated training — though its expected ~4× per-round cost and a specific hypothesis about LayerNorm vs BatchNorm under non-IID skew explaining part of the Keratosis collapse are both documented and ready to test), Phase 5 (src/federated/privacy.py, differential privacy, epsilon/melanoma-sensitivity tradeoff experiments), and a Fitzpatrick-stratified fairness evaluation of the federated model.

Stage 5 — Agentic AI layer Partial

Orchestrator, clinical summarizer, severity triage implemented and tested; treatment advisor, doctor referral, pharmacy locator are empty stubs.

Goal

Turn raw model predictions and explainability output into a patient-facing clinical summary, with deterministic safety rules that the LLM can never override — the project’s core design invariant, stated verbatim in CLAUDE.md: “LLM interprets; Python decides.”

What was built

  • Clinical summarizer (src/agents/clinical_summarizer.py): two-phase design. build_context() is pure Python — deterministic formatting only, no interpretation, and it deliberately omits patient_name. async summarize() calls the Anthropic API (model/max_tokens/effort from configs/agents.yaml; client is injectable for tests) and is instructed to return JSON only. Post-processing after the LLM response: (1) if xai_summary.artifact_flag, downgrade confidence_level one tier via _TIER_DOWN and force recapture_needed=True; (2) if confidence_tier()=='low' and no caveat already mentions model disagreement, append a standard caveat.
  • Severity triage (src/agents/severity_triage.py): triage(predictions, summary, patient) -> TriageResult, a pure function applying three hard overrides after the LLM proposes a severity: melanoma at moderate+ confidence → urgent; model confidence <40% → block + request re-capture; rapidly spreading infection (currently Tinea) → urgent. Rules only escalate or block, never downgrade; escalation and the infection rule are skipped when already blocked. A fourth, config-gated prototype rule (F4, see Stage 2) ships off by default.
  • Orchestrator (src/agents/orchestrator.py): run_screening(image, patient, *, ensemble, explainer, client) -> ScreeningResult sequences quality gate → ensemble → explainer → summarizer → triage, with statuses completed/rejected/blocked. treatment_advice_allowed is False for urgent, referral and re-capture cases — the mechanism that suppresses self-treatment advice on dangerous cases.
  • Schema-level enforcement, a second line of defense beyond the LLM system prompt: ClinicalSummary’s @model_validator enforces urgent → immediate_referral at the Pydantic level — the safety constraint is checked in two independent places.

Tech stack

Anthropic async SDK (anthropic), model Claude Haiku 5.5 (configs/agents.yaml), Pydantic v2 for the ClinicalSummary/Predictions/XAISummary/PatientMetadata contracts, YAML config for model/token/effort settings.

Key design decision: no temperature

Sampling parameters (temperature, top_p, top_k) are rejected on Haiku 5.5 and are a 400 error on every model from API version 2026-10-21 onward. The depth/cost control is output_config={"effort": "low"} instead. Thinking is on by default on this model, so max_tokens must cover thinking plus the JSON body — confirmed live at max_tokens: 1000 sufficient, ~500 output tokens used in practice.

Real-API validation (CLAUDE.md gap 2, done 2026-10-31)

All three --case values of scripts/summarize_one.py (disagreement / artifact / urgent) ran against the real Claude Haiku 5.5 API and returned valid, parseable JSON (3/3). Cost ~$0.0005 per call. Confirmed live: output_config={"effort":"low"} accepted; the disagreement caveat and the artifact-forced recapture_needed both fire as designed; on a melanoma prediction at 0.91 confidence the model returned urgent with immediate_referral: true unprompted — the schema validator never had to reject a response in this run. Access is gated by scripts/_guard.py (SpendGuard), which refuses to run any paid script unless both SKINSURE_ALLOW_SPEND=1 and an explicit --max-usd are set — neither has a money-spending default.

Key design principle: “a derived figure needs its denominator” (PUBLISHABLE_FINDINGS F3)

A confirmed, reproduced defect: build_context() originally passed agreement_score as a bare fraction (“Model agreement: 0.33”). In two independent live runs, the LLM inferred the wrong denominator — “the two models agree at only 0.33” when actually 1 of 3 agreed, and “the two models agree completely” when 3 of 3 did. Fixed by stating the count explicitly (“1 of 3 models agree with the primary label (score 0.33, low tier)”), after which the next run reported correctly. Documented as a general design rule for any LLM-in-the-loop clinical system: a derived number handed to a model needs its denominator stated, or the model will invent one, and the invented value lands in patient-facing text.

Gaps/limitations

  • Three of six planned agents are empty (0-byte) stub files: treatment_advisor.py, doctor_referral.py, pharmacy_locator.py. src/agents/tools/ is an empty directory. src/agents/CLAUDE.md (local design-pattern doc) is also an empty stub.
  • Two residual unknowns in the summarizer’s real-API validation: (1) the artifact case’s final confidence_level: low is ambiguous because _TIER_DOWN maps both moderate→low and low→low — the live run proves the branch executed but not the size of the downgrade (covered instead by a parametrized unit test); (2) nothing has been run against a real lesion photo through the full summarizer — the ensemble and Grad-CAM have been validated on real photos (Stage 2/3), but the LLM leg of that specific path has not.
  • FakeClient (used in unit tests) accepts **kwargs and ignores them, so it cannot catch a request parameter the real API would reject — exactly the class of bug temperature was. One test (test_request_carries_system_prompt_and_context) covers this blind spot by asserting on request shape, but a richer FakeAnthropicClient (scripted malformed JSON/timeout/429/empty content) is explicitly deferred to a later phase (A5) of the production-agent-engineering track.
  • No tool-use, no RAG, no fine-tuning anywhere in the agentic layer — by explicit design decision (Stage 7/A9), not oversight: the task is framed as language generation over already-structured facts, so prompting alone was judged sufficient and cheaper than the alternatives.

Stage 6 — API & deployment / end-user application layer Partial

/predict + /health implemented; /explain//history not built; no Docker, no CI/CD, no cloud deployment, no frontend.

Goal

Expose the screening pipeline over HTTP so a website or client can send an image and metadata and receive a clinical summary; eventually run as a containerized, monitored, publicly reachable service.

What was built

  • api/main.py: create_app(ensemble, explainer, client), exposing GET /health and POST /predict (multipart image + metadata JSON string). The explainer is injected; when the ensemble is a real SkinEnsemble and none is passed, create_app defaults to GradCamExplainer. With no explainer available, /predict returns 503 rather than inventing explainability data.
  • api/schemas/: patient.py (PatientMetadata) and predictions.py (Predictions, XAISummary, Severity, ConfidenceLevel, ClinicalSummary) — these are the API’s request/response contract.
  • Packaging: pyproject.toml (pip install -e .[dev]), .env.example, .gitignore.
  • Scripts for direct, non-HTTP use: scripts/predict_one.py (single-image ensemble inference) and scripts/summarize_one.py (single summarizer call, real-API gated).

Tech stack

FastAPI + Pydantic v2 for the API layer, python-multipart for file upload, uvicorn as the dev server (uvicorn api.main:app --reload). No frontend framework has been chosen — web/ is an empty placeholder in the target architecture with its own stub CLAUDE.md.

Key design decisions

  • Uploaded images are never used for training — a stated invariant held throughout the documentation; images are kept only for the patient’s own history (GET /history, not yet built), and the federated-learning data flow is explicitly one-way (hospitals → model; patient uploads never re-enter training).
  • Secrets never touch git or images — .env locally (gitignored), a secret manager in the cloud (planned, not yet implemented).
  • Planned cloud target: the roadmap specifies deployment to a major cloud provider via Docker, and the separate Production Agent Engineering Track plan recommends Google Cloud Run in asia-south1 (Mumbai) specifically — citing its always-free monthly allowance (180,000 vCPU-seconds / 360,000 GiB-seconds / 2M requests), scale-to-zero behaviour, and Mumbai’s Tier-1 (cheapest) pricing — over AWS App Runner (closed to new customers 30 April 2026) or Fargate (no free grant). This recommendation is a planning document’s analysis, not yet implemented.

Gaps/limitations (all explicitly “not yet created” per CLAUDE.md)

  • Routes not built: GET /history, POST /history, POST /explain.
  • No auth, no rate limiting, no middleware (api/middleware/auth.py, rate_limit.py are planned paths with nothing behind them).
  • No containerization: docker/Dockerfile, docker-compose.yml, .dockerignore do not exist.
  • No CI/CD: .github/workflows/ does not exist; there is no automated test run on PRs and no fairness-regression gate in CI, despite CLAUDE.md stating fairness-as-CI-check as a design invariant.
  • No cloud deployment has occurred — the Cloud Run recommendation above is unexecuted analysis, not a running service. No public or auth-gated URL exists.
  • No structured logging, no production error handling, no monitoring/alerting, no cost dashboard — all planned under ROADMAP Phase 10 and the production-agent-engineering track’s A1/A8 phases, neither of which has shipped code (A1 observability tracing is the one partial exception — see Stage 7).
  • No frontend exists. The README and architecture docs describe a future “User (Web App)” at the top of the system diagram, but no web client has been built or framework-selected.
  • Latency is a known, measured problem for a synchronous endpoint: Grad-CAM alone measured 2.6–6.3s mean, up to 15.7s on the dev CPU (Stage 3) — before the LLM call is even made. The planning documents explicitly flag that a synchronous /predict at this latency “likely needs a timeout and a progress response, not just a looser number,” and leave the p95 latency ship-gate value open, not yet set, pending real end-to-end measurement.

Stage 7 — Observability, cost governance & the Production Agent Engineering Track (A0–A9) Partial

A0 budget/baseline and A1 tracing substantially complete; A2–A9 are a detailed, unexecuted plan.

This track (phases named A0–A9, “Agent-ops”) runs alongside but separately from the ROADMAP’s numbered phases, specifically to take the agentic layer toward production-grade maturity: tracing → evals → guardrails → fallbacks/HITL → security → data governance → LLMOps → systems-design ADRs.

A0 — Budget, API baseline, mocked LLM — Done

  • configs/llm.yaml: price table, tiered by prompt length. Haiku 5.5 is priced by prompt length: prompts up to 100K tokens pay $0.10/$0.50 per MTok input/output; prompts over 100K tokens pay 5× on every category ($0.50/$2.50), and cache reads and writes count toward that 100K threshold — flagged repeatedly across the documentation as the single easiest thing to get wrong when designing a cached prompt.
  • src/utils/costs.py: cost_usd(), unit-tested against hand calculations (17 tests), implementing the two-tier pricing logic.
  • scripts/_guard.py: SpendGuard / SpendRefused / SpendLimitReached, enforcing the explicit-spend requirement described in Stage 5.
  • pytest runs with network disabled (--disable-socket --allow-hosts=127.0.0.1 in pyproject.toml): egress raises SocketConnectBlockedError, verified against a real address and through httpx. Documented honest limit: the guard hooks socket creation/connect(), not DNS resolution — it enforces “no API calls,” not “no packets.”
  • Measured baseline: ~$0.0005/call, ~500 output tokens, 3/3 cases valid JSON (2026-10-31 live run).

A1 — Tracing & observability — Done, 2026-11-03

  • src/observability/redaction.py: the PII boundary. span_attributes() is an allowlist, so patient_name is absent by construction; patient_id is a salted HMAC; medical history is reduced to a count; images are logged as SHA-256 + dimensions, never raw bytes.
  • src/observability/tracing.py: configure_tracing, get_tracer, span(), with every gen_ai.* attribute name centralized in one place (OpenTelemetry GenAI semantic conventions, still at Development stability).
  • run_screening emits six decision spans: screening (root), quality_gate, ensemble, explainer.gradcam, summarizer, triage. The triage span’s rules_fired/severity_before/severity_after attributes are explicitly called out as the safety audit trail proving “rules only escalate or block, never downgrade.”
  • /predict and every error response carry X-Request-ID.
  • Tracing is OFF by default (configs/observability.yaml, exporter: none), and OpenTelemetry is an optional dependency — verified that the app boots and serves with opentelemetry absent (import-blocked test).
  • Measured overhead: a span costs ~250µs against a 2.6–15.7s /predict call; a raising, stalled, or repeatedly-failing exporter leaves the request unaffected while a real exception still propagates.

A2–A9 — planned, not built

The plan document (SkinSure Production Agent Engineering Track...md) specifies, in detail but without implementation:

  • A2 — bootstrapped replay traces (synthetic patients over held-out labelled images) and manual error-analysis/failure-taxonomy building.
  • A3 — classifier/summarizer/end-to-end metrics, a validated LLM-as-judge (with its own TPR/TNR requirement against human labels), and versioned ship gates (e.g. melanoma sensitivity ≥0.90 with 95% CI lower bound ≥0.85; the /predict p95 latency gate is explicitly left OPEN — do not set yet, because Grad-CAM alone has been measured up to 15.7s).
  • A4 — guardrails and least-privilege AgentSpec permissions (an invoke_agent() choke-point wrapper for every agent, present and future).
  • A5 — fallbacks (a deterministic template-summary fallback, circuit breakers, retry policy) and human-in-the-loop review queues.
  • A6 — AI security red-teaming (OWASP LLM/Agentic Top 10 mapping, adversarial-image testing, prompt-injection test corpus).
  • A7 — data pipeline governance: provenance manifest, licence audit (flagging that several source datasets, e.g. HAM10000/ISIC 2019 and DermNet, carry CC-BY-NC or CC-BY-NC-ND licences incompatible with unqualified commercial use), de-duplication via DVC/pHash, and skin-tone fairness evaluation against external tone-labelled datasets (DDI, SCIN, DermaCon-IN) since the training corpus itself has no Fitzpatrick labels.
  • A8 — LLMOps: versioned releases, drift detection, rollback drills, cost-per-query dashboards.
  • A9 — systems-design ADRs and a formal agent plug-in contract for the still-unbuilt treatment advisor / referral / pharmacy agents.

Key design decision, stated as an explicit rule of thumb (A9)

The plan’s “cheapest correct component wins” framework, applied retroactively to justify choices already made in Stages 2–5: lesion classification uses classic ML (fixed label set, calibratable, CPU-cheap) rather than an LLM/VLM; severity triage uses deterministic rules (auditable, injection-immune) rather than an LLM; the clinical summary uses prompting (language generation over already-structured facts, no external knowledge needed) rather than RAG or fine-tuning. Fine-tuning is explicitly rejected for any current component: “no high-volume, high-quality target data; prompting + guards meets the gates.”

Gaps/limitations

  • A2 through A9 are plans, not code. No replay traces exist, no failure taxonomy has been written, no ship gates are enforced in CI, no guardrail/permission layer wraps the summarizer, no fallback/circuit-breaker exists, no red-teaming has been run, no data-governance documents (DATASHEET.md, MODEL_CARD.md) exist, and no production deployment exists for A8 to monitor.
  • The plan itself flags several of its own prior estimates as wrong and already corrected once: an original ~2.3s Grad-CAM latency estimate (from a single synthetic image) was revised to 2.6–15.7s after real-photo testing; an original $0.01 cost-per-query gate was revised down after the real measured ~$0.0005/call baseline showed ~14× slack.
  • The plan is explicit that none of its ship-gate thresholds are clinical standards — “engineering starting points” requiring clinician review before any real-patient use, which has not occurred.

Other findings (PUBLISHABLE_FINDINGS.md)

  • F2 — pHash de-duplication removes 22.3% of the test set (162 of 727 images have a near-duplicate in train/val); the resulting accuracy inflation from contamination was measured at 0.31–0.81pp across seven configurations — real, consistently positive, but smaller than a 22% duplicate rate might suggest.
  • F3 — prompt-context defect (the denominator-omission bug described in Stage 5), reproduced in two independent live runs, then fixed.
  • Methodological note: agreement_tiers.csv is mis-binned and must not be quoted. An analysis notebook compared a float agreement score against the literals 0.33/0.67, but the score can only be exactly 0, 1/3, 2/3 or 1, and 1/3 = 0.3333… ≠ 0.33 — both mixed tiers silently reported n=0, dropping 81 of 565 images from the table. The shipped code is unaffected: Predictions.confidence_tier() uses >= threshold comparisons, not float equality, so this bug exists only in one offline analysis notebook, not in production.

Model generation integrity (v1 vs v2), the other major cross-cutting thread

As detailed in Stage 2, all three original (“v1”) checkpoints failed to reproduce their own stored accuracy when reloaded and re-scored — the project’s single largest data-integrity finding, which motivated a full retrain (“v2”) with an explicit save-then-verify gate (bit-identical tensor reload + re-score within 1e-9 of the stored metric). The v1 checkpoints and an older bare-state-dict set (~479MB) were deleted 2026-11-02 and are not recoverable from git. Generations must never be mixed across backbones — enforced by the app’s checkpoint loader, which refuses to load a mixed set rather than silently combining a v1 and two v2 checkpoints.

The three-test-set discipline

A documented, load-bearing convention repeated verbatim across CLAUDE.md and PUBLISHABLE_FINDINGS.md: three test sets exist (997-image FL split, 727-image contaminated paper set, 565-image de-duplicated clean set) and must never be compared with each other — every figure in this write-up that cites an accuracy or sensitivity number is attributed to its specific basis for this reason.

The honest overall status, in one paragraph

The core screening pipeline is built, measured, and internally self-consistent: three independently retrained and integrity-verified model checkpoints, a documented and tested safety-override layer that the LLM cannot bypass, a measured (not assumed) explainability faithfulness result, and two formally resolved preprocessing A/B experiments with opposite outcomes (one harmful, one inert) that together produced a reusable finding about what separates them. The surrounding production infrastructure — deployment, monitoring, guardrails, security testing, data governance, and three of six planned agents — is either partially built (tracing) or entirely unbuilt (everything else in the A2–A9 track, containerization, CI/CD, and cloud hosting). The project’s own documentation is unusually candid about this distinction throughout, consistently separating “measured” from “planned” and “CONFIRMED” from “PROVISIONAL” rather than blending the two.
